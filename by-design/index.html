Recording script / storyboardSomeone questions your decision, and you hear a question about your worth. That is a moment worth looking at. In The Work Within, I ask you to examine the difference between knowing yourself and performing yourself. Think about a conversation where you wanted the room to confirm that you were capable. What did you do next? Did you listen, explain, withdraw, or agree before you were ready? Choose one of those moments this week. Write down what happened and what you were feeling. When a similar moment comes, try asking which part of the decision needs another look. Then return to your notes. Look at whether you understood the concern more clearly. Bring that observation into the conversation.

Week 3 · Grace

Grace includes refinement: examining what takes priority in your response and practicing what you want to lead with.

W03P01 · A response you regret gives you something to work on.

Loading…Pending approval

A-roll Reel · Suggested 7 days before session · Date unassigned

What did you give priority to, and what would you choose next time?

Recording script / storyboardYou leave a conversation wishing you had handled it differently. Stay with that moment long enough to describe it. In Grace, I use refinement to examine how we respond. Getting better does not require pretending you never feel anger, fear, or frustration. Look at which feeling took priority and what you did with it. Maybe someone interrupted you and you answered sharply. Write down the interruption, your response, and what happened afterward. Then choose what you want to practice when that moment comes again. You might ask the person to finish their concern before you answer. Afterward, look back. What did you notice this time? That gives you something specific to keep working on.

Recording script / storyboardEveryone can see the same problem, and everyone is waiting for someone else to name it. You have probably been in that room. Going First asks you to look at leadership as an action available in a moment. A title can carry authority, but you can ask a useful question before you have one. Think about a meeting that ended without clear ownership. Someone could have asked who would take the next step. That is a place to begin. This week, notice one moment where people are waiting. Offer a question or a first step that fits your responsibility. Make room for other people to contribute. Look back at what followed. Did your action help the group move, or did you take the conversation away from them?

Recording script / storyboardYou introduce another process, but the same argument returns. Before replacing it again, look at how it is being used. This tenet is called It Depends Who's Holding the Rock. The rock can be a tool, a policy, a budget, or authority. The question includes the person using it. Imagine the same policy being used to explain an expectation in one conversation and to shut down a question in another. The document has not changed. The use has. Choose one process in your workplace this week. Observe a specific decision around it. Write down what the system allowed and what the person chose. Then discuss whether you need a different process, a different decision, or both. Keep those questions separate long enough to understand them.

Week 6 · Message Received

Communication is measured by what the other person receives, not only what the speaker intended.

W06P01 · You explained it. What did they understand?

Loading…Pending approval

A-roll Reel · Suggested 7 days before session · Date unassigned

Compare the instruction you gave with the understanding the other person describes. Ask the other person to describe the next step in their own words, and invite them to question yours. What did you change after hearing what was received?

Recording script / storyboardYou finish explaining an assignment, and the work comes back differently from what you expected. You remember being clear. Recording script / storyboardYou value the community when it gives you something. What happens when it asks for your contribution? Build Something brings the work back to what we create with other people. Think about a useful practice your team relies on. Maybe one person prepares the conversation, remembers the commitment, and follows up every time. What happens when that person is absent? Look at what you receive and what you help maintain. Choose one practice worth carrying forward. Ask who wants to help sustain it, and agree on what each of you can contribute. Set a time to look back together. The point is to examine the responsibilities that make a community possible, beyond the benefits of being in the room.
